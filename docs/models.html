Ternary Partitioning of Continental-Scale Water Clarity

Section 6 Hierarchical Bio-Optical Models (models.R)

6.1 Additive Beer–Lambert Model Formulation

We test ten candidate linear models that estimate specific light-attenuation coefficients (\(\alpha_{\text{NAP}}, \alpha_{\text{DOC}}, \alpha_{\text{Chl }a}\)) via ordinary least squares (OLS) with a fixed pure-water attenuation offset \(k_w = 0.04776\text{ m}^{-1}\) and no intercept:

\[k_d - k_w = \alpha_{\text{NAP},g}\,\text{NAP} + \alpha_{\text{DOC},g}\,\text{DOC} + \alpha_{\text{Chl }a,g}\,\text{Chl }a, \qquad z_{SD} = \frac{1}{k_w + \alpha_{\text{NAP},g}\,\text{NAP} + \alpha_{\text{DOC},g}\,\text{DOC} + \alpha_{\text{Chl }a,g}\,\text{Chl }a}\]

Here \(g\) indexes the stratification group: continental USA (Model 1), water-body type alone (Model 2), EPA Ecoregion Levels I–IV without water-type separation (Models 3, 5, 7, 9), or EPA Ecoregion Levels I–IV crossed with water-body type (Models 4, 6, 8, 10). Every modeled group requires \(> 100\) training matchups.

Why We Select Model 7 (Level III Ecoregions) for Continental Ternary Analysis

  1. Strong regional skill and parsimony: Moving from the global model (Model 1, \(R^2 = 0.41\)) to Level III ecoregions (Model 7, 47 groups, \(R^2 = 0.50\)) increases \(R^2\) by \(+0.09\) and improves BIC by \(\Delta\text{BIC} = -53{,}653\), while preserving a median group size of \(n = 687\) (109–121,842).
  2. Physical positivity: In Model 7, 37 of 47 Level III ecoregions (representing 98.04% of modeled training matchups) have strictly positive, highly significant (\(p < 0.001\)) slopes for all three constituents. Subdividing further to Level IV (Models 9–10) pushes median group size down to \(n = 343\text{–}349\)—well below the \(n \approx 1{,}000\) convergence threshold (Figure S1)—for only \(+0.02\text{ to }+0.03\) gain in \(R^2\).
  3. Seamless river–estuary continuity: Separating water types within Level III ecoregions (Model 8 vs. Model 7) adds only \(+0.02\) in \(R^2\) (0.50 \(\rightarrow\) 0.52). Because Model 7 uses a single set of regional slopes across streams, lakes, and estuaries, it can be applied across connected river-to-estuary networks without artificial step changes at water-type boundaries (Figure 3).

6.3 Comparison with Published Bio-Optical Benchmarks

In global Model 1 (\(n = 277{,}382\)), \(\alpha_{\text{DOC}} = 0.0732\text{ m}^2\text{ g}^{-1}\), \(\alpha_{\text{NAP}} = 0.0432\text{ m}^2\text{ g}^{-1}\), and \(\alpha_{\text{Chl }a} = 0.0086\text{ m}^2\ (\text{mg Chl }a)^{-1}\) (ratios \(\alpha_{\text{DOC}} \approx 1.7\,\alpha_{\text{NAP}} \approx 8.5\,\alpha_{\text{Chl }a}\) in Model 1, and \(\alpha_{\text{DOC}} \approx 2.4\,\alpha_{\text{NAP}} \approx 12\,\alpha_{\text{Chl }a}\) across all regional groups in Table S1).
