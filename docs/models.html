Section 6 Hierarchical Bio-Optical Models (models.R)

6.1 Additive Beer–Lambert Model Formulation

All ten candidate models estimate specific light-attenuation coefficients (\(\alpha_{\text{NAP}}, \alpha_{\text{DOC}}, \alpha_{\text{Chl }a}\)) via ordinary least squares (OLS) with a fixed pure-water attenuation offset \(k_w = 0.04776\text{ m}^{-1}\) and no intercept:

\[k_d - k_w = \alpha_{\text{NAP},g}\,\text{NAP} + \alpha_{\text{DOC},g}\,\text{DOC} + \alpha_{\text{Chl }a,g}\,\text{Chl }a, \qquad z_{SD} = \frac{1}{k_w + \alpha_{\text{NAP},g}\,\text{NAP} + \alpha_{\text{DOC},g}\,\text{DOC} + \alpha_{\text{Chl }a,g}\,\text{Chl }a}\]

where \(g\) indexes the stratification group: continental USA (Model 1), water-body type alone (Model 2), EPA Ecoregion Levels I–IV without water-type separation (Models 3, 5, 7, 9), or EPA Ecoregion Levels I–IV crossed with water-body type (Models 4, 6, 8, 10). Because interaction-only terms with no shared main effects form a strictly block-diagonal design matrix, per-group QR decomposition (lm.fit()) is mathematically identical to a joint glm() fit (to machine precision \(\sim 10^{-15}\)) while executing orders of magnitude faster. Every modeled group requires \(> 100\) training observations.

Model Selection Rationale: Why Model 7 (Level III Ecoregions) Is Selected for Continental & Longitudinal Applications

  1. Substantial gain in regional predictive skill and parsimony. Moving from the continental global model (Model 1, \(R^2 = 0.41\)) to Level III ecoregions (Model 7, 47 groups, \(R^2 = 0.50\)) improves \(R^2\) by \(+0.09\) and reduces common-sample BIC by \(\Delta\text{BIC} = -53{,}682\), while maintaining a robust median group size of \(n = 687\) (109–121,842).
  2. Physical positivity and statistical significance across modeled ecoregions. In Model 7, 37 of the 47 Level III ecoregions yield strictly positive, highly significant (\(p < 0.001\)) specific attenuation slopes for all three constituents (\(\alpha_{\text{NAP}} > 0, \alpha_{\text{DOC}} > 0, \alpha_{\text{Chl }a} > 0\)), and none of the non-positive slope estimates in the remaining groups is statistically significant at \(p < 0.01\) (all \(p \ge 0.10\)). In contrast, subdividing to Level IV ecoregions (Model 9, 123 groups, median \(n = 343\)) or Level IV \(\times\) water type (Model 10, 163 groups, median \(n = 349\)) pushes median sample sizes well below the \(n \approx 1{,}000\) stability threshold (Figure S1) and substantially increases the number of small groups with unconstrained negative slope estimates caused by narrow local predictor ranges.
  3. Seamless application across river–lake–estuary continuums. Differentiating by discrete water-body type at the ecoregion level (Model 8 vs. Model 7) increases \(R^2\) by only \(+0.02\) (0.50 \(\rightarrow\) 0.52), and within Model 7 each individual water-body type is well predicted (R² = 0.46–0.54). Because Model 7 does not impose artificial parameter discontinuities at water-body type boundaries, it can be applied continuously across linked river-to-estuary networks (Figure 3).

6.3 Comparison with Theoretical & Field Bio-Optical Benchmarks (Bannister 1974; Kirk 1994)

Across models and water types, fitted specific attenuation coefficients demonstrate strongest per-mass attenuation by DOC, followed by NAP and Chl a. In global Model 1 (\(n = 277{,}382\)), \(\alpha_{\text{DOC}} = 0.0732\text{ m}^2\text{ g}^{-1}\), \(\alpha_{\text{NAP}} = 0.0432\text{ m}^2\text{ g}^{-1}\), and \(\alpha_{\text{Chl }a} = 0.0086\text{ m}^2\ (\text{mg Chl }a)^{-1}\) (ratios \(\alpha_{\text{DOC}} \approx 1.7\,\alpha_{\text{NAP}} \approx 8.5\,\alpha_{\text{Chl }a}\) in Model 1, and \(\alpha_{\text{DOC}} \approx 2\,\alpha_{\text{NAP}} \approx 11\,\alpha_{\text{Chl }a}\) across all regional groups in Table S1). These empirically derived continental coefficients agree closely with first-principles biophysical derivations and local optical studies:
