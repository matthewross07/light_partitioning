Ternary Partitioning of Continental-Scale Water Clarity

Section 4 Analytical Reporting Floors & WQP Physical Plausibility Diagnostics

Multi-agency observational compilations such as the Water Quality Portal (WQP) synthesize measurements from hundreds of federal, state, tribal, and local monitoring organizations. While the AquaMatch harmonization workflow standardizes parameter names, units, and analytical tiers, 1–2% of raw WQP records contain severe field-recording or data-entry anomalies that can heavily leverage ordinary least squares (OLS) regression slopes and depress explained variance (\(R^2\)) if left unscreened.

At the same time, marginal quantile truncation (such as trimming the top and bottom 0.1% or 1% of each variable independently) unnecessarily discards genuine ultra-clear oligotrophic waters (Secchi up to 13.96 m) and real storm-flow sediment or algal bloom events (TSS up to 978 mg/L, Chl a up to 990 µg/L) that obey Beer–Lambert optics. To eliminate recording errors without truncating genuine environmental extremes and without introducing statistical circularity, R/prep.R applies a transparent two-stage protocol to build the final training dataset (n = 277,382):

4.1 Stage 1: Analytical Reporting Floor Substitution (trim_simultaneous())

Rather than dropping low-concentration observations or trimming the 0.1–99.9% marginal tails, non-negative entries reported as 0.0 or below standard analytical Method Detection Limits (MDLs) are substituted via ifelse(x < floor, floor, x) using standard EPA and USGS reporting limits (chl_a = 0.10 µg/L, doc = 0.25 mg/L [EPA Method 415.1 / USGS NWQL], tss = 0.50 mg/L, and secchi = 0.01 m). This retains 100% of the 281,439 non-negative simultaneous 4-variable observations while preventing zero-placeholder entries from distorting log-transforms or ternary proportions:

4.2 Stage 2: A Priori Beer–Lambert Physical Plausibility Screen (clean_wqp_discordance())

4.2.1 Why Joint Optical-Chemical Screening Outperforms Marginal Quantile Trimming

Marginal quantile trimming evaluates each variable in isolation—discarding true clear-water and high-turbidity extremes while failing to catch joint physical contradictions where each individual number looks ordinary in isolation, but their combination violates underwater radiative transfer by orders of magnitude:

  1. Unflagged streambed/lakebed bottom hits in crystal-clear shallow water (3,157 records, 1.12% of simultaneous data): Field crews lower a Secchi disk in a shallow, clear stream or littoral zone (\(0.05\text{–}0.20\text{ m}\) total water depth) where the disk hits the gravel/sand bottom while still clearly visible, and record the water depth (Secchi = 0.05–0.20 m, implying \(k_d = 1/z_{SD} = 5\text{–}20\text{ m}^{-1}\)) without setting a bottom-hit flag—even though simultaneous water chemistry shows optically clear water (\(\text{TSS} < 3\text{ mg L}^{-1}\), \(\text{DOC} < 3\text{ mg L}^{-1}\), \(\text{Chl }a < 2\ \mu\text{g L}^{-1}\), where true \(k_d \approx 0.15\text{–}0.40\text{ m}^{-1}\)).
  2. Unit confusion and bottle/timestamp mismatches (900 records, 0.32% of simultaneous data): Records reporting Secchi = 3–10 m (\(k_d \approx 0.10\text{–}0.33\text{ m}^{-1}\)) alongside extreme particulate or dissolved concentrations (\(\text{TSS} > 100\text{ mg L}^{-1}\) or \(\text{DOC} > 25\text{ mg L}^{-1}\), where true \(k_d > 3\text{ m}^{-1}\)), caused by Secchi depth entered in feet rather than meters or storm-flow sediment samples matched to baseflow clarity readings.

4.2.2 Non-Circular Screening Using Published Optical Constants

To screen out these physically impossible combinations without using our fitted regression models (avoiding any statistical circularity), we compare each observation’s measured diffuse attenuation (\(k_d = 1 / z_{SD}\)) against its a priori canonical Beer–Lambert attenuation (\(k_{d,\text{lit}}\)) computed strictly from independent published bio-optical cross-sections (zero parameters estimated from our dataset):

\[k_{d,\text{lit}} = k_{w,\text{lit}} + k_{\text{NAP}}^*\,\max(0, \text{NAP}) + k_{\text{DOC}}^*\,\text{DOC} + k_{\text{Chl}}^*\,\text{Chl }a\]

where:

  • \(k_{w,\text{lit}} = 0.080\text{ m}^{-1}\) (pure-water attenuation in the transparent window; Kirk 1994; Lee et al. 2015),
  • \(k_{\text{NAP}}^* = 0.040\text{ m}^2\text{ g}^{-1}\) (specific attenuation of non-algal suspended particles; Kirk 1994; Gohin et al. 2005),
  • \(k_{\text{DOC}}^* = 0.070\text{ m}^2\text{ g}^{-1}\) (specific attenuation of dissolved organic carbon; Morris et al. 1995; Griffin et al. 2018), and
  • \(k_{\text{Chl}}^* = 0.016\text{ m}^2\ (\text{mg Chl }a)^{-1}\) (canonical specific attenuation of phytoplankton chlorophyll a; Bannister 1974; Kirk 1994).

Observations where observed \(k_d\) and canonical literature \(k_{d,\text{lit}}\) diverge by more than a factor of \(10^{0.80} \approx 6.31\times\) (\(|\log_{10}(k_d / k_{d,\text{lit}})| > 0.80\)) are flagged as physically discordant and removed (4,057 of 281,439 rows = 1.44%), yielding the final 277,382 training dataset.

4.3 Proof That 100% of True Environmental Extremes Are Preserved

Because clean_wqp_discordance() filters on the ratio between observed and optically expected attenuation rather than cutting off high or low concentrations, genuine hyper-eutrophic blooms (Chl a up to \(990\ \mu\text{g L}^{-1}\)), turbid sediment plumes (TSS up to \(978\text{ mg L}^{-1}\), Secchi down to \(0.01\text{ m}\)), humic blackwater systems (DOC up to \(49.9\text{ mg L}^{-1}\)), and ultra-oligotrophic alpine lakes (Secchi up to \(13.96\text{ m}\)) pass the screen completely intact whenever their Secchi depth and constituent concentrations are physically consistent:

4.4 Diffuse Attenuation (\(k_d\)) and Pure-Water Offset (\(k_w\))

Following the contrast-transmittance theory of Lee et al. (2015, 2018), diffuse light attenuation in the visible transparent window (\(k_d\), in \(\text{m}^{-1}\)) is inversely proportional to Secchi disk depth (\(z_{SD}\), in meters) with proportionality constant \(a = 1\):

\[k_d = \frac{1}{z_{SD}}\]

Every regression model in Section 6 fits non-water attenuation (\(k_d - k_w\)) with no intercept, where \(k_w\) represents baseline attenuation by pure water (\(\alpha_w\)). Following Kirk (1994) and Lee et al. (2015), \(k_w\) is defined a priori from the clearest water in the training dataset (\(\max(z_{SD}) = 13.96\text{ m}\)):

\[k_w = \frac{1}{1.5 \times \max(z_{SD})} = \frac{1}{1.5 \times 13.96\text{ m}} = 0.04776\ \text{m}^{-1}\]

This sets pure water to account for two-thirds of total light attenuation at the maximum observed Secchi depth and is applied consistently both when estimating all ten models and when back-calculating predicted Secchi depth (\(\hat{z}_{SD} = 1 / [\hat{k}_{d,\text{non-water}} + k_w]\)).
