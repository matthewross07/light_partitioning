Section 8 Continental Application & Dominance Regimes (apply_model7())

8.1 Continental 3-LAC Application Dataset (n = 478,929)

Because the calibrated bio-optical model requires only the three constituent concentrations (\(\text{NAP}\), \(\text{DOC}\), and \(\text{Chl }a\)) to partition non-water light attenuation, Model 7 can be applied to every simultaneous 3-LAC sample in the harmonized AquaMatch dataset—with or without a paired Secchi depth measurement—provided that:

  1. \(\text{Chl }a\), \(\text{DOC}\), and \(\text{TSS}\) were measured synchronously at a non-facility monitoring station (with sub-detection entries floored at the standard analytical reporting limits of Section 4);
  2. Each constituent (and Secchi depth, if measured) lies within the empirical range of the training dataset (Section 4) so the regression model is never extrapolated beyond its calibration domain; and
  3. \(\text{NAP} > 0.01\text{ mg L}^{-1}\) so all three constituent concentrations are strictly positive.

8.2 Ternary Attenuation Fractions & Secchi Prediction

For each observation \(r\) assigned specific attenuation coefficients \((\alpha_{\text{NAP}}, \alpha_{\text{DOC}}, \alpha_{\text{Chl }a})\) from its Level III ecoregion in Model 7 (or global Model 1 fallback if the ecoregion has \(\le 100\) observations or a non-positive slope estimate):

\[f_{i,r} = \frac{\alpha_i C_{i,r}}{\alpha_{\text{NAP}}\,\text{NAP}_r + \alpha_{\text{DOC}}\,\text{DOC}_r + \alpha_{\text{Chl }a}\,\text{Chl }a_r}, \qquad i \in \{\text{NAP}, \text{DOC}, \text{Chl }a\}, \qquad \sum_{i} f_{i,r} = 1\]

and total diffuse attenuation and Secchi depth are predicted using the consistent training pure-water offset \(k_w = 0.04776\text{ m}^{-1}\):

\[\hat{k}_{d,r} = k_w + \alpha_{\text{NAP}}\,\text{NAP}_r + \alpha_{\text{DOC}}\,\text{DOC}_r + \alpha_{\text{Chl }a}\,\text{Chl }a_r, \qquad \hat{z}_{SD,r} = \frac{1}{\hat{k}_{d,r}}\]

fd <- get_final_data()

8.4 Hypothesis Tests of Water Clarity Across \(>80\%\) Dominance Classes (\(p < 0.01\))

Reconciling Frequency of DOC Dominance with Magnitude of NAP Turbidity

  1. Frequency vs. intensity of attenuation. Across the continental dataset, DOC is the most frequent primary attenuator (116,567 DOC-dominant samples with mean \(z_{SD} = 1.10\text{ m}\), because even clear headwater streams and oligotrophic lakes contain background humic DOC when suspended sediment and algal concentrations are low). In contrast, when NAP exceeds 80% of non-water attenuation (25,793 samples, typically during storm-flow sediment pulses or in erosive rivers), total suspended mass concentrations are high, yielding the lowest absolute water clarity (mean \(z_{SD} = 0.40\text{ m}\), median \(0.24\text{ m}\)), followed by hyper-eutrophic Chl a-dominant blooms (mean \(z_{SD} = 0.71\text{ m}\), median \(0.38\text{ m}\)), DOC-dominant waters (\(1.10\text{ m}\)), and Mixed waters (\(1.23\text{ m}\)).
  2. All 6 pairwise differences satisfy \(p < 0.01\). Under both Holm-adjusted Welch heteroscedastic \(t\)-tests (\(\max p = 1.49 \times 10^{-4}\)), non-parametric Wilcoxon rank-sum tests (\(\max p = 3.27 \times 10^{-7}\)), and equal-variance Tukey HSD tests (\(\max p = 0.0042\)), all six pairwise comparisons are highly significant (\(p < 0.01\)).
