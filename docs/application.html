Ternary Partitioning of Continental-Scale Water Clarity

Section 8 Continental Application & Dominance Regimes (apply_model7())

8.1 Continental 3-Constituent Application Dataset

Once Model 7 is calibrated on the 274,439 four-variable training matchups, it requires only the three constituent concentrations (\(\text{NAP}\), \(\text{DOC}\), and \(\text{Chl }a\)) to partition non-water light attenuation. Under our asymmetric “odd-one-out” rule (Rule 2), when Secchi depth is much shallower than the three constituents imply (\(z_r > +2.5\text{ SD}\), 5,183 matchups, mostly clear, shallow streams), only the secchi field is set to NA so the 3-constituent chemistry is retained with a modeled Secchi depth. Conversely, records where Secchi depth is much deeper than the constituents imply (\(z_r < -2.5\text{ SD}\), 1,817 records) are dropped. Applying Model 7 across all simultaneous 3-constituent matchups within the training concentration bounds yields 484,528 observations across 39,614 stations. No record is dropped for its NAP value. Where the algal dry mass estimated from Chl a equals or exceeds TSS (4,789 records, 1.0%), the non-algal mass is below what TSS can resolve, so NAP is drawn below the TSS reporting limit of 0.5 mg L⁻¹ in the same way as measured values below their limits (Section 4). Training and application use the same values:

Dataset Subset Observations (n) Unique Monitoring Sites
4-Variable Training Dataset (Secchi + 3 LACs measured, &#124;z_r&#124; <= 2.5 SD) 274,439 22,356
Continental 3-LAC Application Dataset (within training bounds) 484,528 39,614
├── Application matchups with Measured Secchi depth 274,439 22,356
└── Application matchups with Modeled Secchi depth (incl. rescued shallow stream LACs) 210,089 23,741

For each observation \(r\), the non-water light attenuation share of constituent \(i \in \{\text{NAP}, \text{DOC}, \text{Chl }a\}\) and the modeled Secchi depth (\(\hat{z}_{SD,r}\)) are:

\[f_{i,r} = \frac{\alpha_i C_{i,r}}{\alpha_{\text{NAP}}\,\text{NAP}_r + \alpha_{\text{DOC}}\,\text{DOC}_r + \alpha_{\text{Chl }a}\,\text{Chl }a_r}, \qquad \hat{z}_{SD,r} = \frac{1}{k_w + \alpha_{\text{NAP}}\,\text{NAP}_r + \alpha_{\text{DOC}}\,\text{DOC}_r + \alpha_{\text{Chl }a}\,\text{Chl }a_r}\]

Across all 484,528 application matchups, 90.3% (437,375 records) use Level III ecoregion-specific Model 7 slopes and 9.7% (47,153 records) use the global Model 1 fallback.

8.2 Continental Mean Ternary Partitioning by Water-Body Type

Across all 484,528 matchups, average non-water light attenuation is led by DOC (57.7%), followed by NAP (34.0%) and Chl a (8.3%). Streams (57.7% DOC, 35.6% NAP, 6.8% Chl a) and estuaries (56.0% DOC, 37.9% NAP, 6.1% Chl a) cluster tightly along the DOC–NAP axis, whereas lakes (59.5% DOC, 24.1% NAP, 16.4% Chl a) exhibit more than double the phytoplankton attenuation share and span the full ternary triangle:

8.3 Dominant, Majority and Largest Contributor

We use three terms for how strongly one constituent leads non-water light attenuation in a sample, and they are not interchangeable:

  • Dominant: the constituent supplies more than 70% of non-water attenuation.
  • Majority contributor: it supplies more than 50%.
  • Largest contributor: its share is simply the largest of the three, however small the margin.

8.4 Water Clarity Across Dominance Regimes

How does absolute water clarity (\(z_{SD}\)) vary when a single constituent dominates light attenuation? Grouping observations into four dominance regimes based on the 70% threshold (NAP-dominant \(f_{\text{NAP}} > 0.7\), Chl a-dominant \(f_{\text{Chl }a} > 0.7\), DOC-dominant \(f_{\text{DOC}} > 0.7\), and Mixed) reveals an important distinction between frequency of dominance and severity of turbidity:

  • DOC is the most frequent single dominant attenuator (177,926 samples, mean \(z_{SD} = 1.22\text{ m}\)), because background humic DOC absorbs light even in relatively clear streams and lakes when suspended sediment and algal biomass are low.
  • NAP-dominant waters (51,881 samples) have the lowest water clarity (mean \(z_{SD} = 0.60\text{ m}\), median \(0.39\text{ m}\)), followed by Chl a-dominant waters (717 samples; mean \(z_{SD} = 0.86\text{ m}\), median \(0.46\text{ m}\)), DOC-dominant waters (\(1.22\text{ m}\)), and Mixed waters (\(1.24\text{ m}\)).
  • The Chl a-dominant class is small and mixed. Because no record is dropped for its NAP value, it holds two kinds of sample: algal blooms, and low-particle waters where the algal dry mass estimate equals or exceeds TSS and NAP is drawn below the reporting limit. 177 of its 717 samples are of the second kind, and they are clearer than the blooms (mean modeled \(z_{SD}\) 1.31 m against 0.72 m).
