9.3 Figure 3: Longitudinal River–Estuary Continuum Along the Delaware River and Bay

Longitudinal shift in light-attenuation partitioning along 231 km of the Delaware River and Bay across 22 long-term monitoring stations (31DELRBC_WQX, \(\ge 8\) distinct years during 2005–2017). Because Model 7 assigns attenuation slopes by Level III ecoregion without imposing a categorical break between Stream and Estuary, it captures the transition from DOC dominance at the head of tide in Trenton (Site 1, 0 km) through increasing NAP dominance down the tidal river (Sites 2–4, 41–123 km) to DOC–NAP co-dominance and interannual switching driven by wind and tidal resuspension in Delaware Bay (Sites 5–6, 180–231 km).

9.4 Figure 4: Site-Level Temporal Stability — Lucistatic vs. Lucidynamic Regimes

For each station with \(> 7\) simultaneous matchups (9,128 sites across the U.S.), the ternary area fraction (tern_area_fraction()) measures the share of the equilateral ternary triangle enclosed within \(\pm 2\text{ SD}\) of that site’s mean composition. Streams (median 10.2%) and estuaries (median 10.9%) are more temporally compact (lucistatic) than lakes (median 15.7%, lucidynamic). Fewer than 5% of lakes (4.2%), 3% of streams (2.4%), and 1% of estuaries (0.8%) span \(\ge 50\%\) of ternary space.
